Who we are

My Little World: Create & Play is published by Sasano Mizutani, an individual developer based in Sweden. The publisher is responsible for the personal information handled through our support service and this website. On Android, the publisher and Google are also jointly responsible for collecting, on the device, the information Google Play Billing sends to Google, and for sending it, when the app connects to Google Play; see Who is responsible, and on what legal basis, in the Android section.

For support or privacy questions, a parent or guardian can email whimzilab@gmail.com.

The app is designed for young children, with parents and guardians managing purchases and adult settings. This policy describes the iOS and Android apps, including test versions, together with this support website and our support email. It does not cover a browser-based game.

Your child’s saved world

Normal play does not require an account, a child’s name, an email address or a photograph. Mini Me is made by choosing from artwork included in the app. There is no camera or photo-upload feature.

The app saves play on the device, including:

  • Mini Me’s appearance, clothes and favourite outfits, and an unfinished character-creation step.
  • Drawings, other creations, room choices and the positions of decorations.
  • Recycled creations and their recycling dates, plus music and sound preferences.

A local character identifier connects Mini Me with the saved world. It is not an online account. The app does not send this identifier, your child’s drawings or their saved play to the publisher.

The app has no advertising, publisher-operated play analytics, location feature, chat or public sharing. We do not sell children’s saved play or use it for advertising. On Android, the app connects to Google Play only after a grown-up has chosen “Connect to Google Play” in Grown-ups, and then only while Grown-ups is open or to finish a purchase a grown-up started. During those connections, Google Play Billing sends some technical information to Google automatically; see the Android section and the children’s privacy notice. There is no publisher-operated cloud account or cross-device synchronization.

Children’s privacy notice

This notice is for parents and guardians. It explains what information is collected through the app from children, why, and for how long, in the form the US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Rule (COPPA) asks for. It applies to every family, wherever you live. In the app, the “Privacy” button on the welcome screen and the playroom opens a short version of it.

Who collects information through the app

  • We, Sasano Mizutani, respond to all questions from parents about the app and children’s information, including questions about Google’s part. Email: whimzilab@gmail.com.
  • Google, on Android only: Google Ireland Limited for people in the European Economic Area or Switzerland, and Google LLC for everyone else, including people in the United States. Google collects information through Google Play Billing, which Google requires for in-app purchases on Google Play.

On iPhone and iPad, purchases use Apple’s StoreKit, which is part of iOS. Apple handles them under its own privacy terms.

What is collected from children, and why

We collect no personal information from children for our own use. The app asks for no name, email address or photo. It has no chat or public sharing, so a child can’t make personal information public. Drawings, Mini Me and the saved world stay on the device.

On Android, after a grown-up has chosen “Connect to Google Play” in Grown-ups, Google Play Billing sends Google technical information during the app’s connections to Google Play (see the Android section). Under COPPA, the parts that count as personal information are persistent identifiers:

  • the device’s network (IP) address, which Google’s servers see when information is sent;
  • as a precaution, the two random numbers Google Play Billing makes: one for each connection, and one for each purchase attempt.

The other technical details, such as the device model and Android version, go with them. The information includes no name, contact details, photo, voice recording, drawing, saved world or Mini Me identifier, and the app doesn’t use the device’s location services.

We let Google Play Billing collect these identifiers only to support the app’s internal operations, which the COPPA Rule allows without asking for parental consent (16 CFR 312.5(c)(7)). Those operations are:

  • maintaining and analysing how purchases work in the app, including completing, checking and restoring a purchase a grown-up asked for, and finding and fixing errors;
  • network communications with Google Play;
  • protecting the security and integrity of purchases.

Google Play’s Data safety form calls this purpose “analytics”; here it means only these operations.

How we keep these identifiers from being used for anything else

  • A grown-up decides. The app connects to Google Play only after a grown-up chooses to, and then only while Grown-ups is open or to finish a purchase a grown-up started. “Stop connecting to Google Play” ends it.
  • No other software that collects. Apart from Google Play Billing, the app contains no advertising, analytics or social software from other companies, and its own code opens no internet connections. Of the software inside the app, only Google Play Billing sends information off the device. Android itself and Google software on the device can communicate with Google separately, under the device’s settings: for example, device backups and Android System WebView crash reports. The app tells WebView not to send usage statistics.
  • Nothing of your child’s is added. The app doesn’t ask for the advertising ID or hardware identifiers. It adds nothing of your child’s to what Google Play Billing sends, and attaches no account or profile identifier to purchases.
  • No server of ours. We run no server and never receive this information.
  • Checks before each update. Before each Google Play Billing update, we read Google’s release notes and its entry in Google’s SDK Index, and check what the library sends. If an update would send more, we won’t release it until we have dealt with that. If Google says it links this information to accounts or uses it beyond running and improving Google Play Billing, we will stop the app connecting to Google Play, if need be by removing purchases from the Android app. Either way, we will update this notice.
  • What we can’t control. We can’t see or control how Google uses the information once it has it. We rely on Google’s terms, under which data collected through Google Play is handled under Google’s Privacy Policy, and on Google’s statement that this logging is used to improve Google Play Billing’s performance and to support errors. Google’s terms with app developers also let Google use usage statistics from Google Play, in the aggregate, to improve Google Play and other Google products and services.

Sharing

Google Play Billing sends the identifiers above straight to Google (Google Ireland Limited in the European Economic Area and Switzerland, Google LLC elsewhere), the provider of Google Play Billing, for the operations above. Google Play’s Data safety section shows this as data the app “shares”. We don’t disclose children’s personal information to anyone else.

Our data retention policy

  • Why the information is collected: for the internal operations listed above.
  • Why it is kept: we have no business need to keep it, and we don’t. Google says this logging is used to improve Google Play Billing’s performance and to support errors; Google hasn’t said why or how long it keeps it.
  • When it is deleted: we hold none of it, so there is nothing for us to delete. On the device, information waiting to be sent is deleted if it can’t be sent within about seven days, and straight away if you clear the app’s data or uninstall the app. Google keeps what it receives under its own retention practices (how Google retains data). Google has not published a period for this logging. We have asked Google for one and will add it here if Google publishes it.

What you can do

  • Not connect. Everything in the app works without Google Play, except buying and restoring Full World on Android.
  • Stop connecting at any time in Grown-ups. A purchase already verified stays unlocked. Information already waiting on the device may still be sent within about seven days. For a payment that is still waiting to go through, or a purchase the app hasn’t confirmed yet, see Stopping the connection in the Android section.
  • Clear the app’s data or uninstall the app to remove anything waiting on the device. This also removes the saved world on the device, the remembered choice to connect and the purchase record, so Full World stays locked until a grown-up connects again and restores it.
  • Ask Google about information it holds, using the options in Google’s Privacy Policy.
  • Ask us at whimzilab@gmail.com. We answer for our part and, if you agree, pass the rest on to Google.

App Store and Google Play purchases

Where the installed app offers the Full World unlock, purchases and restore controls are in the gated Grown-ups area. Full World is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. Apple handles payment on iOS through the App Store; Google handles payment on Android through Google Play. The app does not ask for or receive your payment-card details.

On iPhone and iPad

The app uses Apple’s StoreKit service to check verified purchase information and whether Full World is owned. These checks do not send a child’s drawings or Mini Me identifier to our own server. We do not operate a purchase-verification server.

Apple processes store purchases, account information and related records under Apple’s App Store privacy information. Apple’s store and operating-system services may communicate with Apple independently of the app’s local saved play.

On Android

Where Google Play purchases are enabled, the app uses Google Play Billing to request product and price information, make purchases, check ownership and restore Full World access.

The app does not contact Google Play until a grown-up chooses to. In Grown-ups, behind the grown-ups-only number question, the Full World panel first shows a short notice and a “Connect to Google Play” button. The notice explains that connecting lets the app show the price and buy or restore Full World. It says that while the app is connected, Google Play Billing also sends Google technical information on its own (described below), and what Google says it uses it for. It also says that the choice is remembered, so the app then connects each time Grown-ups opens and to finish a purchase a grown-up started, until a grown-up stops it. Until a grown-up taps the button, the app does not start Google Play Billing, so nothing is sent to Google Play, or by Google Play Billing to Google. Your child’s play, and everything in the app except buying and restoring Full World, works without it.

The choice is remembered on that device only. It is kept in the app’s private storage, in a place Android leaves out of backups and transfers to a new device. So a new device asks again, and so does this one after the app’s data is cleared or the app is reinstalled.

Once a grown-up has chosen to connect, the app connects to Google Play each time Grown-ups is opened, after the number question. Each opening makes a new connection. It disconnects when the grown-up leaves Grown-ups or the app is closed or moved to the background; if a purchase or a check is still in progress at that moment, it disconnects as soon as that finishes.

There is one exception (unless a grown-up has stopped connecting; see below). Until Google Play has finished a purchase a grown-up started (for example, a payment that is still waiting to go through), the app also checks on that purchase each time it opens or comes back on screen, so such a check can run while a child is playing. If a purchase attempt ends with an error or is interrupted, for example because the app is closed, the app keeps checking in the same way in case the payment went through. If Google Play never confirms such a purchase, these checks end at the first check Google Play answers once three days have passed. At all other times, including while a child is playing, the app does not contact Google Play. It relies on the purchase record kept on the device, if there is one.

Stopping the connection

A grown-up can withdraw the choice at any time with “Stop connecting to Google Play” in the Full World panel. The app then ends the connection (if a purchase or a check is still in progress, as soon as that finishes). It makes no further connections, including checks on a purchase, until a grown-up chooses “Connect to Google Play” again. Information already waiting on the device may still be sent (see below).

A Full World purchase the app has already verified stays unlocked on the device, using the signed purchase record kept there. Until a grown-up connects again, the app can’t show the price or buy or restore Full World.

If you stop connecting while a payment is still waiting to go through, or before the app has confirmed a purchase, the app stops checking on it. Google requires an app to confirm (acknowledge) a purchase within three days of the payment going through. If the app hasn’t confirmed it by then, Google may cancel the purchase and refund it, and Full World will not unlock. If you want the purchase, connect again in Grown-ups so the app can confirm it.

Purchase information

The app receives purchase information from Google Play, including the product identifier, purchase token, purchase state and signed purchase record. It uses this information to verify the purchase and decide whether to unlock Full World. When it asks Google Play about Full World, the app sends the Full World product identifier. When it acknowledges a new purchase, it sends that purchase’s token back to Google Play. The app does not supply your child’s drawings, saved world or local Mini Me identifier to Google Play Billing. We do not operate an Android purchase-verification server or upload these purchase records to a publisher-operated server.

A signed record of an acknowledged Full World purchase is kept in private storage on the Android device. The app uses it to keep Full World unlocked without contacting Google Play: when the app opens, while a child plays, whenever the store is unavailable, and after a grown-up has stopped connecting. This record is separate from saved play and excluded from Android’s normal app backup. It is replaced when ownership is refreshed and removed if a successful store check no longer confirms ownership. Clearing the app’s data or uninstalling it removes this local record and the remembered choice to connect; resetting the world inside the app does not. Google’s own purchase records are managed separately.

Information Google Play Billing sends to Google automatically

Google Play Billing also sends technical information to Google on its own. The app’s use of Google Play Billing (connecting, disconnecting and purchases) sets this off, but the app does not choose what is sent and never receives it. Google Play Billing offers apps no documented setting to turn this off. That is why the app asks a grown-up before it connects, and why a grown-up can stop it connecting.

This happens only while the device is connected to Google Play as described above: while Grown-ups is open (or until a purchase or check started there finishes), or during a check on a purchase a grown-up started. Nothing is collected this way before a grown-up chooses to connect, and nothing new after a grown-up stops connecting. Apart from checks on a purchase, a child playing outside Grown-ups does not set it off. While the device is connected, it happens automatically, with no further step for a grown-up to approve. For example, it happens when the app connects to Google Play, when the connection ends, when a store request fails, and during a purchase. The information is sent in the background, so it may leave the device shortly after the grown-up has left Grown-ups, or later if the device was offline. Google says this logging is used to improve Google Play Billing’s performance and to support errors.

Google has published only a one-line description of this logging: whether store requests succeed or fail, and connection problems. From that description and our own review of the library, the information can include:

  • The device: maker, brand and model, hardware and Android system build details, Android version and total memory.
  • Settings and network: the language and country or region setting; the time zone; the mobile network’s country and operator code from the SIM card; and the type of connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data, and the kind of mobile network).
  • The app: its package name and version, and the Google Play Billing version.
  • Billing activity: which store action ran and whether it worked; Google Play’s result codes and messages, and short error descriptions; how long connecting took and how many retries it needed; whether a purchase attempt finished, failed or was cancelled; and counts of log events that could not be sent, and how much is waiting on the device.
  • Times: when each event happened, and how long the device had been on since it was last started.
  • Random numbers: one is made for each connection: each time Grown-ups opens after a grown-up has chosen to connect, and for each check on a purchase. It is attached to most events and store requests until that connection ends, and a new one is made the next time. Another is made for each purchase attempt. They link events from the same visit, check or purchase attempt. They are not your device’s advertising ID or hardware serial number. The first number is also passed to the Google Play Store app on the device, which is signed in to a Google account. Google has not said whether it links the two.

As with any internet connection, Google’s servers can also see the device’s network (IP) address. If the device is offline, the information can wait on the device. It is deleted if it cannot be sent within about seven days. The app keeps this waiting information out of Android device backups and transfers to a new device.

This information does not include your child’s drawings, saved world or Mini Me identifier. The app adds no name, email address or child account to it. In our review, the library itself does not add the purchase token, order number or price. Some messages in it come from Google Play, and we cannot check all of their contents.

The information goes to Google, not to us. We cannot see it, change it or delete it. You can send questions or requests about it to us or to Google; see Who is responsible, and on what legal basis, below. Any of it still waiting on the device is removed when you clear the app’s data or uninstall the app. Google handles the purchaser’s account, payments and purchase history, as well as this information. Google has not published how long it keeps this Google Play Billing information. We have asked Google for these details and will review this section if Google publishes them. This processing is separate from the child’s locally saved artwork. The app’s lack of advertising or publisher-operated play analytics does not mean that Google’s services never process data.

Android and other Google software on the device

The app shows its screens through Android System WebView, which is part of Android. The app tells WebView not to send usage statistics to Google. That setting does not cover WebView’s crash reports, which Android and Google handle under the device’s own settings for sharing usage and diagnostics with Google. Android and Google services on the device, such as device backup, may also communicate with Google independently of the app, under your device and account settings.

Who is responsible, and on what legal basis

Who is responsible. We and Google are jointly responsible (“joint controllers”) for collecting the information described above on the device and sending it to Google, which the app’s connection to Google Play sets off. We decide whether and when the app connects; Google decides what Google Play Billing collects and sends. For people in the European Economic Area (including Sweden) or Switzerland, the Google company is Google Ireland Limited. For everyone else, including people in the United Kingdom and the United States, it is Google LLC, in the United States. Once Google has the information, Google alone decides how to use it. For that later use, Google is an independent controller under its terms with app developers: the Google Play Developer Distribution Agreement (section 9) and Google’s Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms. Google describes its use in Google’s Privacy Policy and on its page How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Legal basis.

  • What Google Play Billing reads on the device and sends on its own: consent (Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR). A grown-up gives it by tapping “Connect to Google Play” after reading the notice in the Full World panel. Google Play Billing reads details of the device and keeps unsent information on it, and Swedish law allows that only with consent (9 kap. 28 § lagen (2022:482) om elektronisk kommunikation). The same tap is that consent. The choice is meant for a parent or guardian, which is why it sits behind the grown-ups-only number question.
  • Buying, restoring and confirming Full World: when a grown-up buys or restores Full World, the exchanges with Google Play needed to complete, confirm and unlock that purchase are necessary for the purchase they asked for (Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Other store exchanges (showing the price, checking ownership each time Grown-ups opens, checks on an unfinished purchase, and confirming a purchase made outside the app, such as with a promo code): our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)). Our interests are to offer Full World as a one-time purchase through Google Play’s billing system, which Google requires for in-app purchases in apps like this one, and to check that each purchase is genuine and confirmed before Full World is unlocked. Google, which takes part in these exchanges, has its own interest in running Google Play’s billing service. These exchanges also happen only after a grown-up has chosen to connect.

Google’s own purposes and legal bases for its part are described in Google’s Privacy Policy.

Withdrawing consent. Tap “Stop connecting to Google Play” in the Full World panel in Grown-ups. The app then stops connecting, and nothing new is collected. Information already waiting on the device may still be sent to Google within about seven days. Clearing the app’s data removes it, but also removes the saved world and the purchase record. Withdrawing doesn’t undo what was sent before; you can ask Google about it (see Requests below). For a payment that is still waiting to go through, or a purchase the app hasn’t confirmed yet, see Stopping the connection above.

Your right to object. You can object at any time to the store exchanges we base on our legitimate interests (listed above) by emailing whimzilab@gmail.com. Tapping “Stop connecting to Google Play” also ends them.

Transfers. For people in the European Economic Area, the information goes to Google Ireland Limited. Google may transfer it outside the European Economic Area, for example to the United States. Google says it relies on its certification under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, or on the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses, for those transfers. For people in the United Kingdom, the information goes straight to Google LLC in the United States. Google says it relies on the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, or on standard contractual clauses, for those transfers.

Requests. You can send requests about this information to us or to Google. We answer for our part: what the app does on the device and when it connects. Only Google holds the information, and we can’t see, change or delete it. For Google’s part, we tell you how to contact Google or, if you agree, send your request on to Google. Because the information carries only random numbers, Google may need details from you to find it.

How long it is kept. We keep none of it. Information still waiting on the device is deleted after about seven days, or when you clear the app’s data or uninstall the app. Google has not published how long it keeps it.

How this appears on Google Play

Google counts what a library inside an app sends to Google as the app’s own collection. So the Data safety section on this app’s Google Play page lists this information as data the app “collects” and “shares”. It appears there under four headings:

  • Purchase history: purchase-attempt outcomes, and the purchase token sent back to acknowledge a purchase.
  • App activity: connecting to and disconnecting from Google Play, and purchase steps. This is not what your child plays or draws.
  • App info and performance: the technical details above.
  • Device or other IDs: the random numbers above.

Under “Data collected”, each is marked as optional, because nothing is collected until a grown-up chooses to connect, and a grown-up can stop connecting at any time. Google’s form makes us pick purposes from a fixed list. For this logging we chose analytics, the closest choice to what Google says the logging is for. The section also says that we haven’t provided a way to request deletion of this data. That is because we hold none of it and have no way to delete it at Google; if you agree, we can pass a request on to Google. The form doesn’t say when the information is sent; on this app, that is only as described above.

Restoring access

Restore uses the account and store used for the original purchase. An Apple purchase does not transfer to Google Play, or vice versa. Restoring Full World restores access to the collection; it does not recover deleted drawings or transfer a saved world. On Android, restoring needs a grown-up to have chosen “Connect to Google Play” in Grown-ups; see the Android section.

Keeping and deleting saved play

Saved play stays on the device until it is changed, deleted or affected by device storage management. Grown-ups can delete creations, reset Mini Me and favourite outfits, or reset the whole world. Permanent deletion and resets ask for confirmation.

Recycling hides a creation and allows recovery for 30 days. Expired recycled creations are removed when the app next opens. Grown-ups can permanently delete recycled creations sooner.

The operating system may include app data in device backups or transfers, depending on your settings. Deleting play inside the app does not necessarily remove a copy from an existing device backup. Manage those backups through your device and account settings: Apple’s settings on iPhone or iPad, or Google’s and your device manufacturer’s settings on Android. Android backup is enabled in the app; whether data is backed up or transferred depends on the device, operating system and your settings. On Android, information that Google Play Billing has not yet sent is kept out of device backups and transfers.

We cannot retrieve a lost world from a publisher-operated cloud service. Removing the app or clearing its data can lose saved play. Resetting a world does not cancel an App Store or Google Play purchase or delete the store’s purchase history.

På svenska

Appen sparar barnets Mini Me, teckningar och värld på enheten, och vi tar inte emot något av det. Om du säkerhetskopierar enheten kan säkerhetskopian innehålla en kopia, och det som raderas i appen tas inte alltid bort från en befintlig säkerhetskopia. Appen har ingen reklam, inga konton och ingen analys av barnets lek.

Valet sparas bara på den enheten. Googles servrar ser också enhetens IP-adress. Appen kan inte stänga av detta, men inget skickas innan en vuxen väljer att ansluta, och teckningar, den sparade världen och Mini Me-identifieraren ingår aldrig i den tekniska informationen.

Vi och Google Ireland Limited är gemensamt personuppgiftsansvariga för att uppgifterna samlas in på enheten och skickas till Google; hur Google sedan använder dem ansvarar Google ensamt för. Den rättsliga grunden för den automatiska tekniska informationen är samtycke, som du ger genom att trycka på ”Connect to Google Play”.
