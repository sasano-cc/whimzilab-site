Who we are

My Little World: Create & Play is published by Sasano Mizutani, an individual developer based in Sweden. The publisher is responsible for the personal information handled through our support service and this website.

For support or privacy questions, a parent or guardian can email whimzilab@gmail.com.

The app is designed for young children, with parents and guardians managing purchases and adult settings. This policy describes the iOS and Android apps, including test versions, together with this support website and our support email. It does not cover a browser-based game.

Your child’s saved world

Normal play does not require an account, a child’s name, an email address or a photograph. Mini Me is made by choosing from artwork included in the app. There is no camera or photo-upload feature.

The app saves play on the device, including:

  • Mini Me’s appearance, clothes and favourite outfits, and an unfinished character-creation step.
  • Drawings, other creations, room choices and the positions of decorations.
  • Recycled creations and their recycling dates, plus music and sound preferences.

A local character identifier connects Mini Me with the saved world. It is not an online account. The app does not send this identifier, your child’s drawings or their saved play to the publisher.

The app has no advertising, publisher-operated play analytics, location feature, chat or public sharing. We do not sell children’s saved play or use it for advertising. On Android, Google Play Billing sends some technical information to Google automatically when the app connects to Google Play, which happens only while Grown-ups is open or to finish a purchase a grown-up started; see the Android section. There is no publisher-operated cloud account or cross-device synchronization.

App Store and Google Play purchases

Where the installed app offers the Full World unlock, purchases and restore controls are in the gated Grown-ups area. Full World is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. Apple handles payment on iOS through the App Store; Google handles payment on Android through Google Play. The app does not ask for or receive your payment-card details.

On iPhone and iPad

The app uses Apple’s StoreKit service to check verified purchase information and whether Full World is owned. These checks do not send a child’s drawings or Mini Me identifier to our own server. We do not operate a purchase-verification server.

Apple processes store purchases, account information and related records under Apple’s App Store privacy information. Apple’s store and operating-system services may communicate with Apple independently of the app’s local saved play.

On Android

Where Google Play purchases are enabled, the app uses Google Play Billing to request product and price information, make purchases, check ownership and restore Full World access. The app connects to Google Play only after the grown-ups-only number question has been answered and Grown-ups is open. It disconnects when the grown-up leaves Grown-ups or the app is closed or moved to the background; if a purchase or a check is still in progress at that moment, it disconnects as soon as that finishes. There is one exception. Until Google Play has finished a purchase a grown-up started (for example, a payment that is still waiting to go through), the app also checks on that purchase each time it opens or comes back on screen, so such a check can run while a child is playing. If a purchase attempt ends with an error or is interrupted, for example because the app is closed, the app keeps checking in the same way in case the payment went through. If Google Play never confirms such a purchase, these checks end at the first check Google Play answers once three days have passed. At all other times, including while a child is playing, the app does not contact Google Play. It relies on the purchase record kept on the device, if there is one.

The app receives purchase information from Google Play, including the product identifier, purchase token, purchase state and signed purchase record. It uses this information to verify the purchase and decide whether to unlock Full World. When it asks Google Play about Full World, the app sends the Full World product identifier. When it acknowledges a new purchase, it sends that purchase’s token back to Google Play. The app does not supply your child’s drawings, saved world or local Mini Me identifier to Google Play Billing. We do not operate an Android purchase-verification server or upload these purchase records to a publisher-operated server.

A signed record of an acknowledged Full World purchase is kept in private storage on the Android device. The app uses it to keep Full World unlocked without contacting Google Play: when the app opens, while a child plays, and whenever the store is unavailable. This record is separate from saved play and excluded from Android’s normal app backup. It is replaced when ownership is refreshed and removed if a successful store check no longer confirms ownership. Clearing the app’s data or uninstalling it removes this local record; resetting the world inside the app does not. Google’s own purchase records are managed separately.

Information Google Play Billing sends to Google automatically

Google Play Billing also sends technical information to Google on its own. The app’s use of Google Play Billing (connecting, disconnecting and purchases) sets this off, but the app does not choose what is sent and never receives it. Google Play Billing offers apps no documented setting to turn this off, so the app cannot switch it off while Google Play purchases are enabled.

Because the app connects to Google Play only as described above, this happens only while Grown-ups is open (or until a purchase or check started there finishes), or during a check on a purchase a grown-up started. Apart from those checks, a child playing outside Grown-ups does not set it off. Once the app is connected, it happens automatically, with no separate step for a grown-up to approve. For example, it happens when the app connects to Google Play, when the connection ends, when a store request fails, and during a purchase. The information is sent in the background, so it may leave the device shortly after the grown-up has left Grown-ups, or later if the device was offline. Google says this logging is used to improve Google Play Billing’s performance and to support errors.

Google has published only a one-line description of this logging: whether store requests succeed or fail, and connection problems. From that description and our own review of the library, the information can include:

  • The device: maker, brand and model, hardware and Android system build details, Android version and total memory.
  • Settings and network: the language and country or region setting; the time zone; the mobile network’s country and operator code from the SIM card; and the type of connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data, and the kind of mobile network).
  • The app: its package name and version, and the Google Play Billing version.
  • Billing activity: which store action ran and whether it worked; Google Play’s result codes and messages, and short error descriptions; how long connecting took and how many retries it needed; whether a purchase attempt finished, failed or was cancelled; and counts of log events that could not be sent, and how much is waiting on the device.
  • Times: when each event happened, and how long the device had been on since it was last started.
  • Random numbers: one is made each time Grown-ups opens, and for each check on a purchase. It is attached to most events and store requests until that connection ends, and a new one is made the next time. Another is made for each purchase attempt. They link events from the same visit, check or purchase attempt. They are not your device’s advertising ID or hardware serial number. The first number is also passed to the Google Play Store app on the device, which is signed in to a Google account. Google has not said whether it links the two.

As with any internet connection, Google’s servers can also see the device’s network (IP) address. If the device is offline, the information can wait on the device. It is deleted if it cannot be sent within about seven days.

This information does not include your child’s drawings, saved world or Mini Me identifier. The app adds no name, email address or child account to it. In our review, the library itself does not add the purchase token, order number or price. Some messages in it come from Google Play, and we cannot check all of their contents.

The information goes to Google, not to us. We cannot see it, change it or delete it. Questions or requests about this information should go to Google, using the options described in Google’s Privacy Policy. Any of it still waiting on the device is removed when you clear the app’s data or uninstall the app. Google handles the purchaser’s account, payments and purchase history, as well as this information. Google has not published how long it keeps this Google Play Billing information. We have asked Google for these details and will review this section if Google publishes them. This processing is separate from the child’s locally saved artwork. The app’s lack of advertising or publisher-operated play analytics does not mean that Google’s services never process data.

How this appears on Google Play

Google counts what a library inside an app sends to Google as the app’s own collection. So the Data safety section on this app’s Google Play page lists this information as data the app “collects” and “shares”. It appears there under four headings:

  • Purchase history: purchase-attempt outcomes, and the purchase token sent back to acknowledge a purchase.
  • App activity: connecting to and disconnecting from Google Play, and purchase steps. This is not what your child plays or draws.
  • App info and performance: the technical details above.
  • Device or other IDs: the random numbers above.

Google’s form makes us pick purposes from a fixed list. For this logging we chose analytics, the closest choice to what Google says the logging is for. The section also says the data can’t be deleted. That is because we hold none of it and have no way to delete it at Google. The form doesn’t say when the information is sent; on this app, that is only as described above.

Restoring access

Restore uses the account and store used for the original purchase. An Apple purchase does not transfer to Google Play, or vice versa. Restoring Full World restores access to the collection; it does not recover deleted drawings or transfer a saved world.

Keeping and deleting saved play

Saved play stays on the device until it is changed, deleted or affected by device storage management. Grown-ups can delete creations, reset Mini Me and favourite outfits, or reset the whole world. Permanent deletion and resets ask for confirmation.

Recycling hides a creation and allows recovery for 30 days. Expired recycled creations are removed when the app next opens. Grown-ups can permanently delete recycled creations sooner.

The operating system may include app data in device backups or transfers, depending on your settings. Deleting play inside the app does not necessarily remove a copy from an existing device backup. Manage those backups through your device and account settings: Apple’s settings on iPhone or iPad, or Google’s and your device manufacturer’s settings on Android. Android backup is enabled in the app; whether data is backed up or transferred depends on the device, operating system and your settings.

We cannot retrieve a lost world from a publisher-operated cloud service. Removing the app or clearing its data can lose saved play. Resetting a world does not cancel an App Store or Google Play purchase or delete the store’s purchase history.

Contacting support

If you email us, we receive your email address, the name shown by your email service, your message and any attachments you choose to send. We use this information to answer your request, investigate a problem and handle privacy questions. Our basis for this processing is our legitimate interest in providing support and resolving requests, and any applicable legal obligations.

Our support inbox uses Google’s Gmail service. The publisher and the email-service provider handle the correspondence needed to deliver and respond to your message. Please read Google’s privacy policy for information about that service.

Support information is kept for as long as needed to answer and resolve the request, handle related follow-up or meet applicable legal obligations. You can ask us to delete your correspondence. Information that must be kept for a legal obligation or an unresolved legal claim may need to be retained for that purpose.

Please contact us as a parent or guardian. Avoid sending a child’s name, photograph, drawings or other unnecessary personal information. Do not send passwords, verification codes or payment-card details. If unnecessary child information reaches our support inbox, contact us so we can address it and arrange deletion where appropriate.

This website

This website provides support and privacy information for parents and guardians. It has no account registration or contact form. We have not added advertising or analytics scripts. The pages do not ask for a child’s information and do not access saved play in the app.

The website at whimzilab.com is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes, including when visitors are not signed in to GitHub. This hosting information is separate from the iOS and Android apps’ local saved play. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement for its data handling, retention and privacy rights information.

Our email and hosting providers may process information outside Sweden or the European Economic Area. Their privacy policies explain their international processing and safeguards. For questions about information handled through this website or our support service, email whimzilab@gmail.com.

Your privacy rights

Depending on applicable law, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or receive a copy of personal information, restrict its use, or object to processing based on legitimate interests. Email whimzilab@gmail.com to make a request. We may need enough information to verify that the request concerns your information.

Requests about the information Google Play Billing sends to Google should go to Google; see the Android section.

We cannot remotely access or delete your child’s locally saved world. Use the controls in Grown-ups to manage it on the device.

You can raise a concern with the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY) or your local data-protection authority.
